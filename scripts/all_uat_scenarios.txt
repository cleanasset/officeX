Executive Summary
Purpose, problems solved and value
1.1 Purpose
OFFICEX Rent Roll is a Lease-to-Cash SaaS product: the single source of truth for who occupies which space, on what contractual terms, what they have been billed, what they have paid and what is still owed — across conventional leases, managed offices and co-working. It is not a digital copy of an Excel rent roll; it is a transactional data model with a rent roll view on top.
The Lease-to-Cash chain
Property → Building → Space → Occupant → Contract → Rent Roll → Billing → Invoice → Payment → Allocation → Ageing → Forecast → MIS → (Year 2+) Intelligence
It is sold on its own. A landlord, a property management company, an FM company, a managed service provider or a flex-workspace operator can subscribe to OFFICEX Rent Roll without buying the OFFICEX CAFM suite. When they later add CAFM, Leasing CRM or Marketplace, the same records are reused — no migration, no duplicate masters.
1.2 Business Problems Solved
Problem in Indian CRE and flex workspace today
How OFFICEX Rent Roll solves it
Rent rolls in inconsistent Excel files, one per building or per client owner
Canonical schema, guided import, reusable mapping templates
Escalations (e.g. 15% every 3 years) missed, leaking revenue
Rent schedule engine with 90/60/30-day alerts and approval to apply
Lock-in, notice and renewal deadlines noticed too late
Dated clause model driving 365/180/90-day expiry pipeline and alerts
Separate invoices for rent, CAM, electricity, water, parking — but no single view of what a tenant owes
Multi-invoice billing with Pay All / Pay Selected / Pay Individual / partial payments and an allocation engine
Managed office and co-working billed by seats, with CAM/electricity bundled — spreadsheet-driven and error-prone
Seat-based billing models (contracted / occupied / minimum commitment / hybrid) with inclusion rules preventing double billing
PM and FM companies manage many owners' portfolios with no clean owner-level statements
Client accounts, billing entities, management mandates, owner statements and fee calculation
Flex operators cannot see centre profitability (member revenue vs. head-lease rent)
Head leases as payable contracts and centre P&L / break-even occupancy
Investor, lender and board reporting takes days
Month-end snapshots and automated MIS
1.3 Value Proposition
For subscribers: stop revenue leakage, collect faster, see expiry and vacancy risk a year ahead, close the month with reconciled figures, and give owners and occupants a professional branded experience.
For OFFICEX: a low-friction entry product that any CRE or workspace business can buy on its own — widening the addressable market beyond CAFM buyers, creating the natural path to CAFM, Leasing CRM and Marketplace attachment, and building the normalised lease and payment dataset that the Year 2+ intelligence layer depends on.
Consistency with OFFICEX sequencing: Managed Services clients remain the first users (their data is loaded by the OFFICEX team); standalone subscriptions follow once the product is proven on those portfolios. AI capabilities are presented as Year 2+ roadmap only.

Concept, Use Cases, Personas & Billing Models
What a rent roll is, who uses it, and how it is billed
3.1 What Is a Rent Roll?
A rent roll is a schedule, stated as of a specific date, listing every leasable space in a property or portfolio with its occupant, contract dates, area or seats, current rent, recoveries, deposits and status. Vacant spaces appear too, so occupied + vacant always equals the leasable total. In facility management the same records drive service charges (CAM, DG backup, HVAC extended hours, parking). In managed offices and co-working, the rent roll is expressed in seats as well as area.
Three properties guide the design:
As-of-date: the rent roll for 31 March must be reproducible later, even after contracts change — hence versioning and month-end snapshots.
Complete: every space appears exactly once; occupied + vacant = total leasable area (and seats, for flex centres).
Reconciled: billed amounts equal the schedule for the period, with every difference explained (concession, proration, credit note).

Canonical Data Structure & Business Rules
One schema absorbing every source format and serving every segment
4.1 Design Principles
Contract-centric and time-aware. Rent is never a single number on the contract; it is dated charge lines and rent steps, so the rent roll can be computed for any date.
Contractual terms are separate from monthly transactions. Contracts and charges define obligations; invoices, payments and allocations record what happened.
Components and models are configuration. Charge types, billing models, tax rates, escalation types and numbering schemes live in configuration tables — never in code.
Area and seats are explicit. Every area carries its basis (chargeable vs. carpet) and unit; seat quantities carry their seat type.
Multi-tenant and multi-client. Every row has org_id; managed-portfolio rows also carry client_account_id. Row Level Security enforces both.
Source-traceable and versioned. Every record knows its import batch and source row; every change is versioned and audited.
