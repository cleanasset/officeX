P: 1.1 Purpose
P: OFFICEX Rent Roll is a Lease-to-Cash SaaS product: the single source of truth for who occupies which space, on what contractual terms, what they have been billed, what they have paid and what is still owed — across conventional leases, managed offices and co-working. It is not a digital copy of an Excel rent roll; it is a transactional data model with a rent roll view on top.

--- TABLE 13 ---
The Lease-to-Cash chainProperty → Building → Space → Occupant → Contract → Rent Roll → Billing → Invoice → Payment → Allocation → Ageing → Forecast → MIS → (Year 2+) Intelligence
--- END TABLE 13 ---

P: It is sold on its own. A landlord, a property management company, an FM company, a managed service provider or a flex-workspace operator can subscribe to OFFICEX Rent Roll without buying the OFFICEX CAFM suite. When they later add CAFM, Leasing CRM or Marketplace, the same records are reused — no migration, no duplicate masters.

P: 3.1 What Is a Rent Roll?
P: A rent roll is a schedule, stated as of a specific date, listing every leasable space in a property or portfolio with its occupant, contract dates, area or seats, current rent, recoveries, deposits and status. Vacant spaces appear too, so occupied + vacant always equals the leasable total. In facility management the same records drive service charges (CAM, DG backup, HVAC extended hours, parking). In managed offices and co-working, the rent roll is expressed in seats as well as area.
P: Three properties guide the design:
P: As-of-date: the rent roll for 31 March must be reproducible later, even after contracts change — hence versioning and month-end snapshots.
P: Complete: every space appears exactly once; occupied + vacant = total leasable area (and seats, for flex centres).
P: Reconciled: billed amounts equal the schedule for the period, with every difference explained (concession, proration, credit note).

P: 4.1 Design Principles
P: Contract-centric and time-aware. Rent is never a single number on the contract; it is dated charge lines and rent steps, so the rent roll can be computed for any date.
P: Contractual terms are separate from monthly transactions. Contracts and charges define obligations; invoices, payments and allocations record what happened.
P: Components and models are configuration. Charge types, billing models, tax rates, escalation types and numbering schemes live in configuration tables — never in code.
P: Area and seats are explicit. Every area carries its basis (chargeable vs. carpet) and unit; seat quantities carry their seat type.
P: Multi-tenant and multi-client. Every row has org_id; managed-portfolio rows also carry client_account_id. Row Level Security enforces both.
P: Source-traceable and versioned. Every record knows its import batch and source row; every change is versioned and audited.

P: 4.14 Core Business Rules & Formulas

--- TABLE 48 ---
Rule ID | Rule | Formula / logic
F-01 | Area rent | Monthly Rent = Chargeable Area × Rent Rate per sq ft
F-02 | Escalated rent | New Rate = Previous Rate × (1 + Escalation %) — compounding; or Initial Rate × (1 + %) when non-compounding
F-03 | Seat revenue | Seat Revenue = Billable Seats × Contracted Seat Rate
F-04 | Contracted-seat billing | Billable Seats = Contracted Seats
F-05 | Occupied-seat billing | Billable Seats = Occupied Seats (from the monthly seat count)
F-06 | Minimum commitment | Billable Seats = MAX(Occupied Seats, Minimum Committed Seats)
F-07 | Hybrid | Charge = Base Commitment + Additional Seats × Seat Rate + Usage Charges
F-08 | Inclusions (R-40) | If charge.is_included = true, no invoice line is created for that component
F-09 | Metered utility | Charge = (Closing − Opening) × Multiplier × Tariff
F-10 | Proration | Period charge × (days occupied in period ÷ days in period) for part-month start/exit
F-11 | Taxable / gross invoice | Taxable = Σ taxable lines; Gross = Taxable + CGST + SGST (intra-state) or IGST (inter-state)
F-12 | Outstanding (R-52) | Outstanding = Invoice Gross − Allocated Payments − Credit Notes + Debit Notes
F-13 | Partial payment | Paid < Invoice Gross → status partially_paid; balance remains outstanding
F-14 | Ageing | Days Outstanding = Today − Due Date for unpaid balance; buckets 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+
F-15 | Occupancy — area | Occupied Area ÷ Total Leasable Area
F-16 | Occupancy — seats | Occupied Seats ÷ Total Available Seats
F-17 | Economic occupancy | Current Rent ÷ (Current Rent + Vacant Space at Asking Rate)
F-18 | WALE | Σ(Remaining Years × Monthly Rent) ÷ Σ Monthly Rent (by income); same by area. Holding over = 0 years
F-19 | NOI / NOI margin | NOI = Property Revenue − Operating Expenses; Margin = NOI ÷ Revenue
F-20 | Management fee | Fee = Σ fee_rules applied (e.g. 4% × Collections) + GST on fee
F-21 | Owner net remittance | Collections − Management Fee − GST on Fee − Expenses Paid on Owner's Behalf − TDS adjustments
F-22 | CAM true-up | Occupant Share of Actual CAM − CAM Billed to Occupant = Debit (+) or Credit (−) note
F-23 | Centre contribution (flex) | Member Revenue − Head-Lease Rent − CAM/Utilities Payable − Centre Opex
F-24 | Break-even seat occupancy | (Head-Lease Rent + Payables + Opex) ÷ (Average Revenue per Occupied Seat × Seat Capacity)
F-25 | Collection efficiency | Collected in period ÷ Billed and due in period
--- END TABLE 48 ---

P: Every formula F-01 to F-25 must have automated unit tests with the worked values from §13 as known answers.
P: 4.15 Multi-Currency, Future Contracts and Schema Mapping
P: Currency: every amount stored in its transaction currency with an ISO code; daily fx_rate table (RBI reference rate via n8n); portfolio totals converted at the as-of date with rate disclosed; snapshots freeze the rate. Main case: USD contracts in GIFT City IFSC/SEZ.
P: Future contracts and pipeline deals: executed-but-not-started contracts have status future and never enter actual collections until activated. Pipeline deals live in the deal entity (§4.7A). The rent roll offers Current, Contracted (+future) and Forecast (+deals weighted by probability) views. Overlapping active/future contracts on the same area or seats are blocked (R-24).
P: Schema mapping: upload → stage raw rows → detect header and strip totals → map columns via saved mapping_template and synonym dictionary ("Super Area", "SBA", "Chargeable" → chargeable_area) → transform (Indian number format, date formats, sq m → sq ft, text → enum, escalation phrase parser) → explode one source row into property/building/space/occupant/contract/charges/steps/deposit → validate (passed / warning / failed / unmapped) → exception queue → diff → approve → commit. Unmapped source columns are preserved in staging for review, never silently dropped. Source-specific columns never become production schema. Generic mapping rules in §13.4; worked example in §13.5.

P: 6.2 Multi-Tenancy, Multi-Client and Entitlements
P: Shared database, shared schema, row-level isolation. Every table has org_id and client_account_id.
P: RLS read policy: org_id ∈ user's organisations AND (portfolio-wide role OR client_account_id ∈ user's clients) AND (portfolio-wide OR property_id ∈ assignments). Occupant users are restricted to their own occupant_id. Write policies add role capability checks.
P: Entitlements are enforced server-side in API middleware (feature code per route) and in job runners. UI hiding is not a control. Entitlements never affect RLS data visibility — a downgraded feature becomes read-only, not invisible.
P: Standalone guarantee: no Rent Roll query may inner-join to a CAFM, CRM or Marketplace table. Cross-product data is read through optional adapters that return empty when the product is absent (e.g. MeterReadingSource: manual | csv | api | cafm).
P: Never trust client-supplied organisation, client-account or property IDs — every request is authorised server-side against the user's memberships. Cross-organisation administrative access (e.g. OFFICEX support) is time-limited and audited.
P: Service-role jobs run as SECURITY DEFINER functions with explicit org scoping; no service keys in clients.
P: Release gate: automated RLS suite seeds two orgs, two client accounts in one org, and an occupant user, and asserts zero leakage for every role and table.
P: Partitioning: invoice_line, audit history, snapshot lines and meter_reading range-partitioned by month beyond ~5M rows. Key indexes: (org_id, client_account_id, property_id), (org_id, status, expiry_date), (org_id, occupant_id, status) on invoices.

P: 6.6 Security & Compliance

P: Input validation, malware scanning of uploads, rate limiting on APIs and portal, PII minimisation, and controlled (audited, role-limited) export and download.
